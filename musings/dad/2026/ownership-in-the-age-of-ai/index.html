Ownership in the Age of AI

Written for Adam, Daniel, Jack & Phoebe

21 July 2026

Dear Adam, Daniel, Jack and Phoebe,

If there is one thing I hope you never lose, it is perspective.

It is very easy to spend your life chasing the next promotion, the next pay rise or the next milestone, believing that happiness sits just over the horizon. I have met people with enormous wealth who are deeply unhappy, and others with very little who seem genuinely content.

Build a life that you enjoy living first. Success should support that life, not replace it.

The value of university

When I was younger I thought university was mostly about getting a degree. As time has gone on, I have realised that may not even be its greatest value.

University gives you independence, lifelong friendships, exposure to different ideas and, perhaps most importantly, the opportunity to learn who you are.

Never underestimate the value of the people you meet along the way. Relationships often compound just as investments do.

Artificial intelligence changes the equation

As I write this, artificial intelligence is improving at an astonishing pace. Every month it becomes capable of doing work that only highly skilled people could perform a short time earlier.

This does not mean people become worthless. It means intelligence is becoming more abundant.

History tells us that when something becomes abundant, its economic value often falls. When something remains scarce, its value usually rises.

That simple observation has shaped much of my thinking.

What remains scarce?

If intelligence becomes cheap, what cannot easily be copied?

Land. Energy. Trust. Reputation. Great relationships. Strong brands. Sound judgement. Ownership of productive assets.

These things may become relatively more valuable because they remain difficult to create and impossible to manufacture instantly.

AI may write software, draft legal documents and answer questions in seconds, but it cannot own a beautiful piece of land, earn someone's trust over decades or replace genuine character.

Understand value

One of the most useful questions you can ask is:

Why is this valuable?

Ask it about companies.

Ask it about property.

Ask it about people.

Ask it about your own time.

The better you understand value, the better decisions you will make.

Own more than you consume

If I have one piece of financial advice, it is this.

Try to become an owner, not simply an earner.

Own businesses.

Own shares.

Own property.

Own ideas.

Own things that continue creating value while you sleep.

There is nothing wrong with earning a salary, but salaries usually stop when you stop working. Ownership has the potential to compound for decades.

Use AI, but do not surrender your judgement

AI will become an extraordinary tool. Learn how to use it well.

But never confuse knowing an answer with understanding it.

Your judgement, your curiosity and your values will always matter more than your ability to produce information quickly.

Build a reputation

Reputation compounds quietly.

Keep your word.

Be dependable.

Admit mistakes.

Help people without immediately asking what you get in return.

Opportunities often arrive through trust rather than talent.

Invest patiently

The world will always tempt you to chase the next exciting thing.

Most wealth is built through patience rather than brilliance.

Learn about compounding.

Invest consistently.

Give good decisions time to work.

Think for yourself

The world is full of confident opinions.

Many people speak with certainty when they should speak with curiosity.

Read widely.

Change your mind when new evidence appears.

Try not to become emotionally attached to being right.

Finally

If you remember only one thing from this letter, let it be this.

Build a life you enjoy. Own more than you consume. Stay curious.

Everything else is simply detail.

Love,

Dad

A question to think about

If intelligence becomes abundant, what do you think becomes truly scarce, and how would that change the way you choose to spend your life?

Context

Written in July 2026 at the age of 53 while living in Winchester, Hampshire and leading PassKit. AI had begun to transform software, business and knowledge work, prompting me to think differently about the future of value, ownership and opportunity.
